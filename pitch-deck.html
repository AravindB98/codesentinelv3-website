The problem2 / 13

AI reviewers produce findings nobody can trace

More code, less scrutiny

A 2025 Veracode study reported that 45% of AI-generated code samples failed security tests.5 Teams are adding AI reviewers to cope.

No record of why

An AI finding arrives without the model version, prompt or evidence that produced it. It cannot be re-derived, so it cannot be audited.

No way to compare

Vendors quote false-positive reductions measured on their own data. A buyer has no controlled way to test the claim on their own code.

How verification works4 / 13

Four checks, no model calls

1 · Chain

Has any statement been altered, removed or reordered?

2 · Records

Does every cited model response still hash to what was recorded?

3 · Replay

Does re-running on the recordings give exactly the same findings?

4 · Evidence

Does every finding point at lines that exist?

It proves a finding is the unedited, reproducible output of a named model and code version. It does not prove the finding is true. That is what the labelled evaluation measures.

Evidence so far5 / 13

What our own pilot does and does not show

Established

Every stored result from the April 2026 pilot is reproduced from its recordings. In one real-model run on 10 samples, false positives were 30 for a single prompt and 1 for the pipeline, with equal recall.

Weaker than it looked

The pipeline returned nothing in 2 of 3 runs that day. 20 of the 30 false positives were extra findings on flawed samples. The two arms used different prompts.

Not done yet

The evaluation on a hand-verified dataset with replicates, on a hosted model and an open-weight model. 204 candidate pairs are collected and waiting for verification.

We publish this because a product about verification has to pass its own test.

Why now7 / 13

Money and regulation are both moving toward checkable AI output

Capital

Positioning9 / 13

The audit trail for AI code review

Who buys first

Application security leads deciding whether an AI reviewer earns its cost. Regulated teams that need a record for automated findings and code that stays on their machines.

What they get

A controlled comparison on their own code with intervals, and findings that an auditor can re-derive without trusting the vendor.

Why it survives a null result

If the pipeline does not beat a single prompt, the rig and the provenance layer are still useful. Only the "fewer false positives" claim would go.
